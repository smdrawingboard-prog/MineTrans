Business Interruption Coverage

Downtime doesn't pause your obligations. Wages, debt service and fixed costs continue whether the shaft is producing or not — business interruption cover is how that gap gets funded.

Why BI Dominates Mining Losses

Business interruption at mining facilities has steadily increased, consistently accounting for about 80% of total gross mining losses. Driven by operational incidents — including fires, machinery breakdowns and geotechnical failures rather than natural disasters — these losses have worsened due to rising commodity prices and ageing global supply chains.

BI Assessment Deliverables

A robust business interruption insurance assessment for a mining facility includes:

  • Description of the mining process and critical operations
  • Asset criticality ranking and production bottlenecks
  • Maximum Foreseeable Loss (MFL) scenario
  • Gross Profit calculation based on policy wording
  • Production interruption and recovery model
  • Increased Cost of Working assessment
  • Supply chain and utility dependency analysis
  • Recommended indemnity period with justification
  • Recommended Business Interruption sum insured
  • Scenario-based BI loss estimates
  • Key assumptions, exclusions and sensitivity analysis

Tailings Dam Liability

Tailings storage facility (TSF) failures carry environmental, social and balance-sheet consequences that standard liability cover doesn't reach.

Coverage is structured through specialised liability, property and environmental policies, underpinned by detailed risk assessment and strict adherence to compliance frameworks.

Why a TSF Loss is Never a Single-Line Claim

TSF failure → property damage, third-party injury, environmental contamination, production shutdown, business interruption, rehabilitation liability — one incident, six consequence categories.

Tailings Risk Assessment Factors

  • Dam breach assessments for improved understanding of catastrophic risk exposure
  • Enhanced protection of downstream communities and the environment
  • Better-informed underwriting decisions and more accurate pricing
  • Stronger organisational governance and accountability
  • Early detection of deteriorating dam conditions
  • Reduced likelihood and severity of catastrophic tailings failures

Types of Tailings Storage Facilities

Tailings are the finely ground waste left after minerals are extracted from ore. How a facility is raised and confined materially changes its stability, cost, water demand and failure consequence.

Typical Information Requested for Each Facility:

  • As-built drawings and original design basis
  • Construction method and raise history
  • Deposition rates and water balance records
  • Independent stability and inspection reports
  • Downstream consequence classification
  • Emergency preparedness and response plan
